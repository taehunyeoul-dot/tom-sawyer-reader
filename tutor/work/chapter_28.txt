CHAPTER 28 (XXVIII) — An Attempt at No. Two—Huck Mounts Guard

[28.0.0] That night Tom and Huck were ready for their adventure.
[28.0.1] They hung about the neighborhood of the tavern until after nine, one watching the alley at a distance and the other the tavern door.
[28.0.2] Nobody entered the alley or left it; nobody resembling the Spaniard entered or left the tavern door.
[28.0.3] The night promised to be a fair one; so Tom went home with the understanding that if a considerable degree of darkness came on, Huck was to come and “maow,” whereupon he would slip out and try the keys.
[28.0.4] But the night remained clear, and Huck closed his watch and retired to bed in an empty sugar hogshead about twelve.

[28.1.0] Tuesday the boys had the same ill luck.
[28.1.1] Also Wednesday.
[28.1.2] But Thursday night promised better.
[28.1.3] Tom slipped out in good season with his aunt’s old tin lantern, and a large towel to blindfold it with.
[28.1.4] He hid the lantern in Huck’s sugar hogshead and the watch began.
[28.1.5] An hour before midnight the tavern closed up and its lights (the only ones thereabouts) were put out.
[28.1.6] No Spaniard had been seen.
[28.1.7] Nobody had entered or left the alley.
[28.1.8] Everything was auspicious.
[28.1.9] The blackness of darkness reigned, the perfect stillness was interrupted only by occasional mutterings of distant thunder.

[28.2.0] Tom got his lantern, lit it in the hogshead, wrapped it closely in the towel, and the two adventurers crept in the gloom toward the tavern.
[28.2.1] Huck stood sentry and Tom felt his way into the alley.
[28.2.2] Then there was a season of waiting anxiety that weighed upon Huck’s spirits like a mountain.
[28.2.3] He began to wish he could see a flash from the lantern—it would frighten him, but it would at least tell him that Tom was alive yet.
[28.2.4] It seemed hours since Tom had disappeared.
[28.2.5] Surely he must have fainted; maybe he was dead; maybe his heart had burst under terror and excitement.
[28.2.6] In his uneasiness Huck found himself drawing closer and closer to the alley; fearing all sorts of dreadful things, and momentarily expecting some catastrophe to happen that would take away his breath.
[28.2.7] There was not much to take away, for he seemed only able to inhale it by thimblefuls, and his heart would soon wear itself out, the way it was beating.
[28.2.8] Suddenly there was a flash of light and Tom came tearing by him: “Run!” said he; “run, for your life!”

[28.3.0] He needn’t have repeated it; once was enough; Huck was making thirty or forty miles an hour before the repetition was uttered.
[28.3.1] The boys never stopped till they reached the shed of a deserted slaughter-house at the lower end of the village.
[28.3.2] Just as they got within its shelter the storm burst and the rain poured down.
[28.3.3] As soon as Tom got his breath he said:

[28.4.0] “Huck, it was awful!
[28.4.1] I tried two of the keys, just as soft as I could; but they seemed to make such a power of racket that I couldn’t hardly get my breath I was so scared.
[28.4.2] They wouldn’t turn in the lock, either.
[28.4.3] Well, without noticing what I was doing, I took hold of the knob, and open comes the door!
[28.4.4] It warn’t locked!
[28.4.5] I hopped in, and shook off the towel, and, Great Caesar’s Ghost!”

[28.5.0] “What!—what’d you see, Tom?”

[28.6.0] “Huck, I most stepped onto Injun Joe’s hand!”

[28.7.0] “No!”

[28.8.0] “Yes!
[28.8.1] He was lying there, sound asleep on the floor, with his old patch on his eye and his arms spread out.”

[28.9.0] “Lordy, what did you do?
[28.9.1] Did he wake up?”

[28.10.0] “No, never budged.
[28.10.1] Drunk, I reckon.
[28.10.2] I just grabbed that towel and started!”

[28.11.0] “I’d never ’a’ thought of the towel, I bet!”

[28.12.0] “Well, I would.
[28.12.1] My aunt would make me mighty sick if I lost it.”

[28.13.0] “Say, Tom, did you see that box?”

[28.14.0] “Huck, I didn’t wait to look around.
[28.14.1] I didn’t see the box, I didn’t see the cross.
[28.14.2] I didn’t see anything but a bottle and a tin cup on the floor by Injun Joe; yes, I saw two barrels and lots more bottles in the room.
[28.14.3] Don’t you see, now, what’s the matter with that ha’nted room?”

[28.15.0] “How?”

[28.16.0] “Why, it’s ha’nted with whiskey!
[28.16.1] Maybe all the Temperance Taverns have got a ha’nted room, hey, Huck?”

[28.17.0] “Well, I reckon maybe that’s so.
[28.17.1] Who’d ’a’ thought such a thing?
[28.17.2] But say, Tom, now’s a mighty good time to get that box, if Injun Joe’s drunk.”

[28.18.0] “It is, that!
[28.18.1] You try it!”

[28.19.0] Huck shuddered.

[28.20.0] “Well, no—I reckon not.”

[28.21.0] “And I reckon not, Huck.
[28.21.1] Only one bottle alongside of Injun Joe ain’t enough.
[28.21.2] If there’d been three, he’d be drunk enough and I’d do it.”

[28.22.0] There was a long pause for reflection, and then Tom said:

[28.23.0] “Lookyhere, Huck, less not try that thing any more till we know Injun Joe’s not in there.
[28.23.1] It’s too scary.
[28.23.2] Now, if we watch every night, we’ll be dead sure to see him go out, some time or other, and then we’ll snatch that box quicker’n lightning.”

[28.24.0] “Well, I’m agreed.
[28.24.1] I’ll watch the whole night long, and I’ll do it every night, too, if you’ll do the other part of the job.”

[28.25.0] “All right, I will.
[28.25.1] All you got to do is to trot up Hooper Street a block and maow—and if I’m asleep, you throw some gravel at the window and that’ll fetch me.”

[28.26.0] “Agreed, and good as wheat!”

[28.27.0] “Now, Huck, the storm’s over, and I’ll go home.
[28.27.1] It’ll begin to be daylight in a couple of hours.
[28.27.2] You go back and watch that long, will you?”

[28.28.0] “I said I would, Tom, and I will.
[28.28.1] I’ll ha’nt that tavern every night for a year!
[28.28.2] I’ll sleep all day and I’ll stand watch all night.”

[28.29.0] “That’s all right.
[28.29.1] Now, where you going to sleep?”

[28.30.0] “In Ben Rogers’ hayloft.
[28.30.1] He lets me, and so does his pap’s nigger man, Uncle Jake.
[28.30.2] I tote water for Uncle Jake whenever he wants me to, and any time I ask him he gives me a little something to eat if he can spare it.
[28.30.3] That’s a mighty good nigger, Tom.
[28.30.4] He likes me, becuz I don’t ever act as if I was above him.
[28.30.5] Sometime I’ve set right down and eat with him.
[28.30.6] But you needn’t tell that.
[28.30.7] A body’s got to do things when he’s awful hungry he wouldn’t want to do as a steady thing.”

[28.31.0] “Well, if I don’t want you in the daytime, I’ll let you sleep.
[28.31.1] I won’t come bothering around.
[28.31.2] Any time you see something’s up, in the night, just skip right around and maow.”
